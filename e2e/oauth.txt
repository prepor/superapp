# The Gmail sign-in, as far as a script can go: reach the add-account panel,
# press the Google button, and read the line the shell puts back on the
# panel. A run never leaves for a browser, so the flow refuses itself and
# says so — and that refusal is exactly the plumbing worth proving here: the
# button resolves, its action reaches the shell, and the shell's answer
# lands on the panel's own status line rather than in a toast alone.
#
# The consent round trip itself cannot be scripted (it wants Google and a
# human). What can be is unit-tested next to the code: PKCE against the
# RFC's vector, the consent URL, the id_token read, the XOAUTH2 envelope
# byte for byte, and a bearer-token sync and send against the fake world.
wait 700
key cmd 2
wait 400
type "settings"
wait 300
key enter
wait 600
click "add account"
wait 600
shot g1-add-panel
click "sign in with google"
wait 800
shot g2-refused
# The line is a hit of its own, so this click only resolves if the panel is
# really saying it — the assertion is the click, not the screenshot.
click "sign-in needs a real run"
wait 300
shot g3-line
quit
